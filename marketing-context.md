## 2. Who buys this

No first-party data yet. Personas below are inferred from the catalogue and
category norms 🔴 — treat as a starting hypothesis to be replaced by real
purchase data after the first ~50 orders.

### P1 — "Kit Upgrader" (primary)
- 25–40, US, works from home or hybrid, mid income
- Buys: laptop stands, charging stations, power banks, mice
- Job to be done: *"my desk setup is a mess and my gear is aging"*
- Buying trigger: friction — a dead battery, a cluttered desk, a new laptop
- Objection: "is this the cheap version of a real product?"
- Beats objection with: specs, real photos, warranty + returns visible

### P2 — "Gift & Impulse" (secondary)
- 22–45, browses on phone, buys in one session
- Buys: LED speakers, smart watches, RGB mice, doorbell cameras
- Job to be done: *"this looks cool and it's under $50"*
- Buying trigger: visual — the product looks good in motion
- Objection: "will it arrive in time?"
- Beats objection with: honest delivery window stated before checkout

### P3 — "Home Security Starter" (highest value)
- 30–55, homeowner or long-term renter
- Buys: doorbell cam, WiFi cameras, smart plugs
- Job to be done: *"I want to see who's at my door without a $200 Ring bill"*
- Buying trigger: an event — a package theft, a trip coming up
- Objection: "no subscription trap? does it actually work?"
- Beats objection with: no-subscription framing, night-vision proof, setup ease
- **This is the best ad audience: highest AOV and highest profit per order.**

## 3. Positioning

**For** people upgrading everyday tech on a real budget,
**Emporx is** a curated electronics store
**that** sells a tight, vetted range instead of an endless catalogue,
**unlike** marketplace sellers with 40 identical listings and no support,
**because** every product is chosen, specced, and backed with warranty and
30-day returns.

**One line:** *Reliable tech, honestly priced, clearly explained.*

**What we do NOT claim 🟢:** fast shipping, US warehousing, brand-name
equivalence, or "limited stock" scarcity we cannot substantiate. Fake
urgency is the fastest way to lose a store like this to chargebacks.

## 4. Voice

Plain, specific, unhyped. Short sentences. Lead with the problem, name the
spec, state the price. No exclamation marks, no "GAME CHANGER", no emoji
walls. The tone is a knowledgeable friend, not an infomercial.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "20,000mAh. Charges an iPhone 15 about four times." | "MASSIVE capacity!!! 🔥🔥" |
| "Ships in 2–3 days, arrives in 7–10." | "Lightning fast delivery!" |
| "30-day returns. We pay return shipping on faults." | "100% RISK FREE GUARANTEE" |

## 5. Unit economics 🟢

Mean gross margin ≈ 51% → **breakeven ROAS ≈ 1.96**.
After ~3% payment fees, **true breakeven ROAS ≈ 2.1**. Target ≥ 2.5.

Max allowable cost per acquisition, by product, if the order is a single item:

| Tier | Products | Profit/order | Max CPA at 2.5x |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Advertise** | WiFi Video Doorbell, Y90 GPS Watch, 30000mAh Bank, WiFi Security Cam, Auto-Track Cam, T11 Earbuds | $23–38 | $15–23 |
| **Retarget/email only** | P22 Watch, Waterproof Earbuds, S07 Earbuds, Fitness Watch | $18–24 | $11–14 |
| **Never advertise cold** | Everything under $30 retail | $10–16 | under $10 — unreachable on Meta |

**The core constraint 🟢:** typical Meta cold-traffic CPA for this category
runs $25–40. Only the top tier can absorb that, and only barely. Cheap SKUs
are not ad products — they are **AOV builders** (cross-sell, bundles, the
$75 free-shipping threshold) and **email products**.
